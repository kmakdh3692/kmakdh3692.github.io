\subtitle{Perceptrons, Sigmoid Neurons, and Artificial Neural Networks}
\author{Donghyun Ko}
\date{2026-01-03}

\begin{document}
\maketitle


This post walks step by step through the foundations of neural networks,
starting with the perceptron as a linear binary classifier that makes
decisions via weighted sums, bias terms, and hard thresholding. It shows
how perceptrons define hyperplane decision boundaries, implement logic
gates such as NAND, and even construct digital circuits like a
half-adder---while also revealing why step activations fail for
gradient-based learning. The story then transitions to sigmoid neurons,
replacing discontinuous switches with smooth, differentiable activations
and deriving their gradients explicitly to explain how learning via
calculus becomes possible. Along the way, the notes connect artificial
neurons to their biological inspirations and formalize how neurons are
stacked into multilayer feedforward networks. The post culminates in the
mathematical formulation of ANNs, clarifying depth, layers, activations,
and why even shallow networks can approximate complex functions.
